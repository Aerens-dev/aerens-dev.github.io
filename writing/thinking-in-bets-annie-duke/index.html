Thinking in Bets

Annie Duke

30 August 2026 · book notes · decision-making

Twenty years of professional poker, after a start in cognitive psychology. One argument: we grade decisions by how they turned out, and for anything involving luck that is the wrong scoreboard.

Resulting

Judging a decision by its result. Pete Carroll’s pass from the one-yard line was defensible on the merits until it was intercepted, and then it was the worst call in the history of football.

Poker, not chess

Chess is computation. Poker is incomplete information plus variance — and so is hiring, investing, medicine and picking an architecture.

Outcome = decision quality + luck

Separate the two or you learn nothing from either.

Wanna bet?

Offer someone real money against what they just asserted and their confidence moves within a second. The trick is asking yourself first.

Seventy per cent, not yes

Hardest to argue with, easiest to move. And it makes changing your mind free: if you said seventy and the other thing happened, you were calibrated, not wrong.

Three, minimum

Self-audit has a low ceiling. What gets past it is people who have agreed in advance that accuracy beats comfort.

The charter

Duke borrows the norms of science — Merton’s CUDOS — as the constitution for such a group.

Don’t contaminate the room

Lead with the outcome and everyone reasons backwards from the answer. Lead with your own view and you have replaced eight opinions with one.

The future is not in the room

Ten minutes, ten months, ten years. Bind the future self while the present one is thinking clearly, and feel the regret before the decision rather than after.

Draw the tree first

Name the futures and weight them in advance, and no outcome is a surprise you have to explain away.

Tilt

The last hand still being played during the next one. The countermeasure is not composure, it is a stopping rule written down before you need it.

Check yourself

One question per idea on this page, 11 in all. Pick an answer and it tells you why. Nothing is scored and nothing is stored.

  1. 1A team ships a risky migration on a Friday and nothing breaks. What does resulting say about how this will be judged?

    Not quite. This is the error rather than a description of it. A Friday migration that happened to survive is a bad decision with a good outcome — the cell nobody investigates.

    Not quite. That is what we would like to happen and almost never does. The whole point of naming the error is that a good outcome closes the inquiry.

    Right. That is exactly resulting: the outcome answers the question and nobody re-opens the decision. Duke's opening example is a pass call that was defensible until it was intercepted.

  2. 2What makes a decision poker-shaped rather than chess-shaped?

    Not quite. Chess has an opponent too. The difference is not competition, it is what you cannot see and cannot control.

    Not quite. The opposite — poker is where the systematic thinking pays. Von Neumann built game theory on poker precisely because bluffing and incomplete information are the interesting parts.

    Right. Chess has near-perfect information and almost no variance, which is why outcome and quality line up there. Hiring, investing and picking an architecture do not.

  3. 3Of the four combinations of decision quality and outcome, which one almost never gets examined?

    Not quite. This gets examined and teaches roughly the right lesson, though by accident rather than by reasoning.

    Not quite. Deserved, and unexamined — but harmlessly so. The dangerous unexamined cell is the one where bad reasoning was rewarded.

    Not quite. This one gets examined constantly — usually too harshly. It is the cell people misfile as a mistake, but it does get looked at.

    Right. Nothing prompts the review. It paid, so it goes in the win column and the reasoning that produced it survives intact to be used again.

  4. 4What does offering someone a real bet on their assertion actually do?

    Not quite. The amount is beside the point. What matters is that any real stake converts an assertion into a probability.

    Not quite. It is not a rhetorical weapon. Used that way it produces defensiveness rather than an estimate, which is the opposite of what it is for.

    Right. Stakes turn a stated position into an estimate. Duke's move is to import the question into your own head before anyone else asks it.

  5. 5You said 70% and the other thing happened. On Duke's account, what were you?

    Not quite. Stating a confidence is not hedging. A 70% claim is a real claim and can be badly calibrated — it just cannot be judged from a single outcome.

    Right. This is the whole payoff of stating confidence: it removes the ego cost of updating, because being on the wrong side of a probability is not being wrong.

    Not quite. One outcome tells you almost nothing about a 70% estimate. Treating it as a verdict is resulting applied to your own forecasting.

  6. 6Why does Duke put the minimum size of a truthseeking group at three rather than two?

    Not quite. Breadth is not the constraint. The problem is that agreement between two people is indistinguishable from agreement about the truth.

    Not quite. The group is not for voting. It is for producing disagreement that would not otherwise surface, and a vote would suppress exactly that.

    Right. A pair can agree for reasons that have nothing to do with the evidence, and nothing in the pair can detect that. The third is the mechanism.

  7. 7In Merton's CUDOS, what does universalism ask of a group?

    Not quite. That is closer to a description of consistency. Universalism is specifically about discounting a claim by its source.

    Right. Which in practice means considering an argument the same way whether it came from the most junior person in the room or the person who runs it.

    Not quite. That is organised scepticism, the last of the four — a norm the group holds rather than a temperament somebody has.

    Not quite. That is communism, the first of the four. A verdict without its evidence cannot be checked by anyone.

  8. 8You want a group's honest read on a decision that has already played out. What do you withhold?

    Right. Both contaminate. Told the result first, everyone reasons backwards from it; told your view first, you have replaced several opinions with one plus agreement.

    Not quite. Worth doing, and it is universalism rather than contamination. The two things that poison the analysis are the outcome and your own stated position.

    Not quite. That is the one thing they need. Without it they are guessing rather than evaluating.

  9. 9What does moving regret in front of the decision buy you?

    Right. Felt afterwards it is just a cost. Felt beforehand it is a signal about a decision you can still change.

    Not quite. Not reliably, and caution is not the goal. Ten-minute regret and ten-year regret often point in opposite directions.

    Not quite. It does not, and does not claim to. The outcome may still be bad; the point is that the feeling is useful earlier.

  10. 10Duke recommends both backcasting and a premortem. What makes the premortem the more useful half?

    Not quite. Neither exercise is primarily about the numbers. They are about naming futures in advance so no outcome has to be rationalised afterwards.

    Right. The obstacle is social rather than analytical. Assuming failure has already happened licenses the objection that nobody would otherwise raise.

    Not quite. A tidy saying, and not the argument here. Both directions produce useful information; only one of them gets around the taboo.

  11. 11What is the countermeasure to tilt?

    Right. Composure is not available at the moment you need it — that is what tilt means. The rule has to be written while you are still capable of writing one.

    Not quite. Recognition is necessary and not sufficient. Tilt is precisely the state in which your own judgement about whether to continue is unreliable.

    Not quite. Still a decision made while tilted. The pre-agreed rule works because it was made by a version of you that was not in the state.
