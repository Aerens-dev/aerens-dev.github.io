Business Finance

The numbers you are accountable for

Not a CFO course and not valuation. This is management accounting and budget control, with exactly enough financial accounting underneath that accruals make sense.

It is written for the person sitting in a steering committee hearing “we’ll accrue it against the control budget” and wanting to know precisely what was just claimed — and, one step further, which part of it is a fact, which is a policy choice, and which is a negotiation.

Before you start

  • Reading a table is not the gap, so there are no bookkeeping drills and no double entry. The course is heavy on why a number is the number it is.
  • Examples use IFRS, which is what Belgian listed companies and most larger Belgian groups report under. Where Belgian GAAP diverges materially — development-cost capitalisation, and provisions — the tutorial says so rather than quietly picking one.
  • Every tutorial is built to about 30 minutes: a concrete failure, three to five ideas with a figure each, one worked example carrying real numbers, something to do, and a self-check.

Where this comes from

Horngren, Datar & Rajan, Cost Accounting: A Managerial Emphasis, 17th ed.
The standard text, and the spine for cost behaviour, flexible budgets and variance analysis.
Anthony & Govindarajan, Management Control Systems, 12th ed.
Budgets treated as a control system rather than a forecast. Source for track 4.
Berman & Knight, Financial Intelligence, rev. ed. (HBR Press)
The accessible entry, and unusually honest about how much of accounting is estimate.
Merchant & Van der Stede, Management Control Systems, 4th ed.
The modern successor to Anthony; the incentives half of track 7.
Kieso, Weygandt & Warfield, Intermediate Accounting
Mechanics reference for accruals, provisions and revenue recognition.
Penman, Financial Statement Analysis and Security Valuation, 5th ed.
Accrual accounting taken seriously rather than as bookkeeping.
Brealey, Myers & Allen, Principles of Corporate Finance
NPV, IRR and the cost of capital for track 6.
PMI, PMBOK Guide, and The Standard for Earned Value Management (2019)
Where cost baseline, contingency reserve and management reserve are actually defined.
AACE International RP 10S-90, Cost Engineering Terminology
Free PDF. The definitions behind track 4, and evidence that they are not universal.
Jensen, “Paying People to Lie”, European Financial Management 9(3), 2003
The argument that a budget tied to a bonus is a machine for producing dishonest numbers.
Hope & Fraser, Beyond Budgeting (HBS Press); Bogsnes, Implementing Beyond Budgeting, 2nd ed.
The critique and the practical version of it.
Sloan, The Accounting Review 71(3), 1996
Evidence that the gap between accrual profit and cash carries information.
CIMA P1 and P2; ACCA PM; MIT Sloan 15.501 and 15.514; Wharton’s Introduction to Financial Accounting
The syllabuses tracks 4 and 5 are shaped after. The MIT and Wharton courses are free.
